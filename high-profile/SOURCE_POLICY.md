# Source policy

Prioritize authoritative public records while retaining uncertainty:

1. IAEA, UN, treaty bodies, regulators and official government material.
2. SIPRI, FAS/Nuclear Notebook and established nonproliferation institutions.
3. Peer-reviewed literature, universities and established think tanks.
4. Reputable investigative and mainstream reporting.
5. Other public materials retained for investigation.

Authority is not truth. Official material establishes an official statement; underlying claims may require independent verification. Lower-tier sources cannot silently replace an assessment. Shared authorship and shared publisher groups do not establish independent corroboration.

Every source retains a title, canonical HTTPS URL, publisher, publication date, language, document type, dataset version, lifecycle, archival description and rights metadata. Snapshots add retrieval time, method, exact hash scope and notes. An archived or stale publication is visibly historical. Absence from this selective collection is not evidence of absence in the world.

Source health supports ACTIVE, STALE, DISCONTINUED, ARCHIVED, SUPERSEDED and UNKNOWN. The seed makes no live endpoint-health claims. Its 2025 SIPRI chapter is marked STALE, its older chapter SUPERSEDED, historical primary documents ARCHIVED, and fictional documents UNKNOWN. Health transitions require append-only lifecycle work in the later Watch phase; current immutable source metadata cannot be silently overwritten.
